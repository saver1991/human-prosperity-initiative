## 1. Legal seat is not where impact happens

A *future* England-and-Wales CIO or charitable company could have **worldwide permissible beneficiaries and research scope** but must comply with each jurisdiction's legal, ethical, banking and data safeguards. A local registered NGO/university/public authority could deliver lawful work under a **signed monitored contract**; the UK trustees would remain responsible for proper funds/use. An alternative future model is an eligible university or nonprofit **fiscal host** which assumes explicitly agreed legal and grant responsibilities. Setting up a country branch/subsidiary is **not assumed**, and cannot be replaced by calling an individual volunteer an in-country partner.

| Model | Who has authority? | Upside | Unresolved real-world gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UK legal recipient + independently due-diligenced local contracted partner | Future UK entity/board signs; local lawful entity delivers scope under written monitoring agreement | May limit country-establishment overhead while drawing on local capability | Local partner/activity registration; purpose, sanctions, tax, grants, participant/data/welfare laws; written contract and proof of proper use |
| UK legal recipient with a direct foreign operating office / employees | Future registered legal party with country-law advice and permission | More operational control if programme demand justified | Direct local registration/licence, employer/payroll, visas, establishment/tax, insurance and data presence: unknown |
| Existing academic/charitable fiscal host + properly approved local consortium | Host signatories and local lawful institutions, not HPI's unincorporated account | May offer research accountability and infrastructure | **No real host/contact**, responsibility/overhead/IP, local ethics, legal funder eligibility and exit terms |
| Current unincorporated HPI open-science project | Founder controls only public documentation and approved technical repository | Low-risk desk work, transparent source and method | **No signed field/research/data/financial authority** anywhere |

Primary basis: [Charity Commission international risk guidance](https://www.gov.uk/charities-how-to-manage-risks-when-working-internationally/), [funds due diligence and end-use](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charities-due-diligence-monitoring-and-verifying-the-end-use-of-charitable-funds/chapter-2-due-diligence-monitoring-and-end-use-of-funds).

## 4. Written partner due diligence / agreement design (T03)

A hypothetical **future** partner intake must be human-owned and restricted to need-to-know records; public GitHub contains only non-identifying descriptions of the workflow.

1. **Selection, non-duplication:** identify who is already serving this population (e.g., Our Newham Money) and what HPI might add; record competing, existing, non-HPI services before proposing anything.
2. **Verify identity, registration and fit:** current registry, governance, ownership/controllers, audited accounts or proportional financials, previous delivery, references, safeguarding record, conflicts and sanctions. Never treat self-asserted alignment as verification.
3. **Evaluate programme and country risk:** harmful effects, beneficiaries' rights, poverty/age vulnerability, local community challenge, eligibility and legal basis, hidden fees and political capture. Record counterarguments and redlines.
4. **Document agreement before authority:** objects, purpose, geographic/beneficiary eligibility, roles, signatories, limits of delegation, donor restrictions, payment tranches, invoices, independent monitoring, IP and publication of adverse results, data-controller and safeguarding responsibility, incident and whistleblowing route, records, audit access, jurisdiction and exit.
5. **Track outputs separately from outcomes:** signed grant contract, restricted-fund code, bank confirmation, budget and FX rate/fees; milestone evidence, reconciled ledger; independent evidence of actual intervention effects rather than beneficiary anecdotes alone.
6. **Disburse only with verified authorisation and dual approval:** no informal cash recipient substitutions, ad-hoc personal accounts or prepayment without safeguards. Monitor use, suspend on unexplained variance or harm, preserve legal reporting.
7. **Close / exit with independent accountability:** final restricted fund reconciliation, assessment of returned balances/asset transfer terms, data destruction/retention, feedback of affected communities and independently challenged public results (redacted).

Charity Commission [Chapter 2 due diligence / verification](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charities-due-diligence-monitoring-and-verifying-the-end-use-of-charitable-funds/chapter-2-due-diligence-monitoring-and-end-use-of-funds) says trustees remain accountable when partners deliver work. See [current UK Sanctions List (updated 3 Nov 2026)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-uk-sanctions-list); this is the operative official designation list, not the retired OFSI legacy consolidated list. Regulatory lists can change before actual engagement.

## 5. International cost, FX and risk rules (T06)

- Begin with **GBP accounting base** only once a legal recipient has actual accounts; capture transaction currency, fee, exchange rate, transaction/settlement date, bank evidence and final partner-received amount. Compare **realised vs budgeted** FX at monthly close.
- Maintain grant-level **restricted balances**, accrual/cancellation liability, 13-week cashflow and supplier invoice matching. Never count unpaid pledges as available cash; do not treat partner claims as impact.
- Proposed **risk limits — not active permissions**: monthly variance review by future finance owner; material FX movement, bank-detail switch, sanctions alert, unexplained transfer/receipt discrepancy, partner safeguarding incident or unverifiable output → hold further transfer pending independent resolution. Specific monetary thresholds need future board/host adoption.
- Distinguish **UK charity eligibility to exist** from each funder's eligible country, nationality of lead, community scope and local beneficiary limits. A UK charity may legally act internationally but may not qualify for a local-only England grant for an overseas activity.
- Forecast Brazil BRL / Italy EUR costs with scenario analysis and source-specific local quotes once actual partner/field budgets exist; no invented FX estimates or legal tax exemptions here.
