# F0-04.2 — International HPI legal structure, charitable objects and constitutional options V0.1

**Prepared 7 November 2026 — decision support only, not counsel advice, an adopted constitution, entity formation or a registration application.** HPI remains a public open-source initiative, with **no appointed trustees, signed fiscal host, organisational bank account or research authorisation**.

## Executive recommendation: two-stage, conditional route

**NOW: HOLD organisational commitments while testing a qualifying existing charity/university/research-institution fiscal host**, since HPI has no funded programme or accountable local research partner and may need a host for eligible restricted grant administration and independent science. A host is **not presumed available** and no outreach/contract is implied.

**IF validated long-term organisational independence and UK charity suitability warrant it: preliminary preference for an England-and-Wales *Foundation CIO***, subject to a real trustee group, legal charity-purpose test, public benefit and operational budget. **Switch to an Association CIO** if community members genuinely require statutory voting membership beyond trustees. **Charitable company limited by guarantee** is a viable alternative when company/legal arrangements demand it. **CIC** is not a substitute for charity registration or charity-restricted grant eligibility; consider it later only for credible trading/product revenue. An open-source community can contribute without automatically being a legal voting membership.

**No founder decision to incorporate or appoint trustees is inferred.** The choice, jurisdiction, tax treatment, regulatory filings, host responsibility and objects must be ratified after qualified charity-law and finance review. [Charity Commission CC22a](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/charity-types-how-to-choose-a-structure-cc22a); [registration guidance](https://www.gov.uk/setting-up-charity/register-your-charity).

## 1. Real-world legal-form comparison

## 2. Charitable objects versus the broader movement mission

The **long-horizon aspiration** to study and possibly redesign economic mechanisms must **not** become an ambiguous constitutional purpose of changing government policy, laws or the economic system. Under England-and-Wales charity law, **all stated purposes must be exclusively charitable and for public benefit**. Political-purpose campaigning cannot itself be a charitable object, though properly justified nonpartisan research and campaigning ancillary to charitable purposes may be possible.

Potential charitable purpose heads:
1. **Prevention/relief of poverty** for public benefit, with evidence-based assistance/services, grants only within safeguards and independently assessed outcomes.
2. **Advancement of education** for public benefit through rigorous independent research on poverty, economic opportunity and prosperity measurement, openly sharing useful findings.

Charity Commission sources: [Charitable purposes](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charitable-purposes/charitable-purposes), [How to write charitable purposes](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-write-charitable-purposes), [research as advancement of education](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/example-charitable-objects/example-objects-advancement-of-education), [CC9 campaigning restrictions](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/speaking-out-guidance-on-campaigning-and-political-activity-by-charities-cc9).

### Draft E&W objects clause — **COUNSEL-REVIEW ONLY, NOT FOR FILING**

> **The objects of the CIO are, for the public benefit, in any part of the world where it may lawfully operate:**
>
> **(1)** The prevention or relief of poverty, in particular but not exclusively by supporting evidence-based initiatives and charitable services that improve access to resources and opportunities for persons experiencing poverty or financial hardship, subject to appropriate safeguards.
>
> **(2)** The advancement of education in the causes, measurement and evidence-based prevention or relief of poverty and related barriers to economic opportunity, in particular but not exclusively by commissioning or conducting independent research, evaluating interventions, and publishing useful research results for public benefit.

**Counsel questions:** Is the second head sufficiently precise, relevant to near-term activities and distinguishable from generic economic policy advocacy? Should there be *one* narrower object for launch? Is charitable poverty relief sufficiently clear when HPI initially provides research tools rather than direct assistance? Does each proposed worldwide programme satisfy public benefit and local law? What limits on private benefit, founders/trustees, open-source IP, restricted grants, collaborations and commercially provided services should be in the model constitution? Are proposed activities genuinely advancing education, and are research results accessible to the public? Will a fiscal host's objects encompass the scoped work?

**Constitution remains to be produced from the official template**, not this excerpt. [Foundation/Association model constitutions](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/setting-up-a-charity-model-governing-documents); [CC22b](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-write-your-charitys-governing-document-cc22b). Charities Act 2011 Part 11 specifies CIO constitution and E&W principal office requirements.

## 3. Governance design and independence gates (proposed, not appointments)

- Aim for **at least three capable independent trustees** for a future charity and collectively cover finance, charity law, research/evaluation, safeguarding, international delivery and lived experience, without alleging anyone has accepted the role. Final trustee number and independence policy depend on the governing form.
- Separate founder/technical stewardship from independent scientific conclusions, research protocol approval, confidential personal-data decisions and finance disbursements. Open-source PR merge is not board or ethics approval.
- Disclose conflicts; independently approve and minute any related-party contracts. **No presumption that trustees or founder can receive salary or trustee remuneration** without lawful authority, governing-document checks and independent decision. See [Charity Commission CC11](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charities-paying-a-trustee-or-a-connected-person-understand-the-rules-cc11).
- Require documented grant restrictions, dual spending approvals, financial statements, audit/examination obligations as applicable, public-benefit reporting and independent research access. Regulatory thresholds change; confirm filing thresholds at actual registration. [2026 threshold changes](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charity-accounts/threshold-changes-at-a-glance).
- No public safeguarding intake of claimant details until a verified accountable human officer and secure channel exist ([F0-04.1 #26](https://github.com/saver1991/human-prosperity-initiative/issues/26)).

## 4. International mandate is distinct from domicile

**A CIO headquartered in England/Wales can pursue worldwide charitable objects**, but it is *not licensed by that fact alone* to operate, fundraise, employ, contract, transfer sensitive data or run studies in every foreign jurisdiction.

Before country-specific implementation: check local NGO/foreign grant registration, finance/foreign-funding restrictions, tax and payroll, sanctions and anti-bribery, partner end-use monitoring, secure banking, research ethics, safeguarding, data transfers, local informed consent and whether specific restricted grants permit activity there.

Approach for the first stage: work through an **actual** authorised local institution only after due diligence and a written division of responsibilities. References: [CC international risks](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/charities-how-to-manage-risks-when-working-internationally), [due diligence and end use of funds](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charities-due-diligence-monitoring-and-verifying-the-end-use-of-charitable-funds/chapter-2-due-diligence-monitoring-and-end-use-of-funds).

## 5. Founder/legal decision matrix and incorporation checklist

**Stage A: HPI may proceed with publicly sourced research and open software** as a clearly non-incorporated collaboration. No money movement, charity claims, partner claims or participant processing.

**Stage B: test fiscal hosting**, verify one real eligible host's charity/research standing and ability to contract, operate internationally and sponsor ethically reviewed Newham work. Any contact or binding negotiations require separate founder authorisation and carefully scoped representation.

**Stage C: if independence justified**, qualified lawyer reviews objects, public benefit, geographic coverage, registration pathway, CIO model constitution, name/availability and principal office; recruit real eligible trustees; finance professional reviews 24-month budget; obtain founder **INCORPORATE / HOST / HOLD** decision under [F0-04.7 #51](https://github.com/saver1991/human-prosperity-initiative/issues/51). Prepare statutory filings only upon explicit approval.

**Stage D: separate research ethics and lawful data readiness.** No organisational legal form alone approves Newham fieldwork or lawful access to DWP/council records ([F0-05 #11](https://github.com/saver1991/human-prosperity-initiative/issues/11), [RT-GATE-01 #67](https://github.com/saver1991/human-prosperity-initiative/issues/67)).

### Adviser question pack (explicitly unresolved)

1. Does E&W charity law recognise the exact near-term poverty-relief plus open research activities as exclusively charitable public benefit? Will the scientific economic-systems research produce publicly useful results without a prohibited political object?
2. Are host-led, Foundation CIO or Association CIO arrangements most proportionate to the actual first authorised study, charity grant criteria and future community governance?
3. What are legal exposures and permissions for an Italian founder resident in the UK, including trustee/director/employee conflicts, remuneration, IP and any cross-border working? **Do not infer personal tax conclusions from citizenship**.
4. What privacy, participant/data-controller, professional indemnity, insurance and research sponsorship responsibilities must sit with an actual accountable institution?
5. How will foreign research and grants be structured so trustees retain beneficiary protection, auditing and end-use verification?
6. Which version-specific registration, account/return/examination, tax, name, anti-money-laundering and donor rules will apply **at the eventual filing date**?
