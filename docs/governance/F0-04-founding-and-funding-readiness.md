# F0-04 — Founding and funding readiness: international HPI organisational roadmap

**Decision:** Founder approved **planning priority P0 · NOW** on 6 November 2026. **Status:** proposed organisational design and research only, **no registration**, grant application, funding received, statutory trustee appointment, project partner, commercial investment or outreach.

## Objective
Make Human Prosperity Initiative a credible, legally accountable and sustainable **international public-benefit organisation**, capable of raising eligible unrestricted/core and restricted/programme resources, supporting independent research and contracting lawful partners worldwide. A possible **England and Wales legal domicile is not a geographical limitation on research or beneficiaries**. The exact form and objects are **subject to professional legal advice and explicit founder decision**.

## Initial organisational options
1. **Foundation CIO** or **association CIO** (England and Wales), depending on desired trustees-only versus wider voting membership; these are possible legally incorporated charities, not existing HPI structures.
2. **Charitable company limited by guarantee** (UK); compare dual filings, governance requirements, costs and member rights.
3. **Fiscal sponsorship / hosting by a real eligible existing charity or research institution**, by written agreement; host legal duties, ownership, data responsibilities and grant restrictions must be clear.
4. **CIC** as an alternate social-enterprise legal form, **not interchangeable with registered charity** or charity tax status. Consider only for a validated commercial-service need.
5. **Remain a public open research collaboration temporarily** until forming an entity solves a verified contracting/funding need.

UK Charity Commission [structure guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/charity-types-how-to-choose-a-structure-cc22a), [charity registration requirements](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-register-your-charity-cc21b), [international charities risk guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/charities-how-to-manage-risks-when-working-internationally).

## International operations: distinction between legal home and beneficiary location
**A UK CIO is not restricted to UK-only activities.** The Charity Commission describes international activities through local organisations and direct charitable work. But the UK charity trustees remain responsible for funds and effective partner checks, and each country may impose separate laws on foreign NGO activity, foreign funding, taxation, grant making, employment, travel, data processing and public benefits delivery. Do **not** assume a UK charity number confers foreign operating permission or eligibility for local grants. The eventual constitution should expressly permit worldwide charitable activities **only where lawfully authorised**.

References: [Charity Commission international risk guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/charities-how-to-manage-risks-when-working-internationally); [trustee end-use of funds and partners](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charities-due-diligence-monitoring-and-verifying-the-end-use-of-charitable-funds/chapter-2-due-diligence-monitoring-and-end-use-of-funds); [charitable registration jurisdiction](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/how-charity-registration-decisions-are-made-charity-commission/how-the-charity-commission-makes-charity-registration-decisions--2).

## Sequencing and milestones
- **Stage A — desk preparation (can be done without external permissions):** legal-form and charitable-purpose analysis, draft constitution, proposed governance, hypothetical budget, verified funding eligibility catalogue and country-compliance checklist. AI-assisted output is **not** regulated legal/financial advice.
- **Stage B — independent challenge and decision:** competent UK charity lawyer, finance/accountant and qualified international programme/ethics expertise; no invented approvals or appointed people. Founder reviews a reasoned options package. Existing charter F0-01 must be harmonised.
- **Stage C — conditional implementation:** only after explicit instruction, recruit qualified trustees/partners, apply for registration or secure appropriate host, establish lawful bank/account controls and fundraising permissions. A formal entity may be required by a particular funder; a signed fiscal host agreement may sometimes be an alternative.
- **Stage D — research/field readiness:** restart F0-02.3 decision and F0-05 planning with real authority, ethics, lawful data, community safeguards, partner review and explicit budgets. **No study/pilot automatically authorised.**

## Funding distinctions
- Separate **unrestricted organisational grants/donations** (sustaining teams, governance, software, essential research) from **restricted project funds** (a defined approved intervention, geography, target population and deliverables).
- Catalog grants, donations, programme sponsorship, publicly procured research contracts and legally appropriate mission-related financing **without treating ordinary investor equity as charity fundraising**.
- A founder's employment compensation, trustee benefit, related-party contracts, charity tax claims and donor restrictions require independently reviewed lawful arrangements. No grants, sponsorships, balances or funding offers exist merely because they appear as prospects in a pipeline.
- Maintain a zero-award operating scenario and explicit no-cost/no-credit limits during planning.

## Hard stop conditions
No collecting public donations or grant applications in the name of a nonexistent entity; no partner/grantee claims; no allocation of participant or donor data to open GitHub/Neon stores; no automatic bank account, contract, legal filing or international operations. A research/funding plan is **not** proof of charity status or an authorised scientific intervention. No country or population was approved by re-prioritising F0-04.
