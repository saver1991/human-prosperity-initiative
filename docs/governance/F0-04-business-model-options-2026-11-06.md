# F0-04.8 — HPI business-model alternatives: an open hypothesis

**Date:** 6 November 2026. **Decision status:** DEFERRED; options recorded for future review only. **No legal structure selected, no company registered, no donor or investor approach authorised.** Parent programme: [F0-04](../roadmap.md), issue [#54](https://github.com/saver1991/human-prosperity-initiative/issues/54).

## Strategic question

Could a mission-locked **for-profit or hybrid** model complement or outperform a conventional nonprofit in delivering HPI's internationally applicable research, reusable tools, and measurable prosperity outcomes? The desired beneficiary outcome matters more than assumed ideology or organisational category; commercial success is not a proxy for human prosperity.

HPI should pursue **research and the tools that make research possible**, while retaining the separation between:
- public-interest **research evidence and scientific independence**,
- reusable **open-source tools, datasets, methods and infrastructure**, and
- any lawful **commercial services, hosted software, contracts or financing** that might sustain them.

## Options to examine later

| Option | Potential revenues / resources | Trade-off to evaluate | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nonprofit / UK CIO or charitable company | Eligible grants, philanthropy, research contracts, service fees where permitted | Restrictions on charitable purposes, application eligibility, governance, donor control and no private distribution of profit | Undecided |
| For-profit mission-driven Ltd | Institutional SaaS/research-service sales, public procurement, contracts and possible equity financing | Mission drift, public-benefit accountability, tax and capital terms, potential loss of charity-restricted grants | Undecided |
| Community Interest Company (CIC) | Trading and some impact-oriented capital/grants | Asset lock, dividend restrictions depending on form, regulator requirements; not interchangeable with registered charity | Undecided |
| Hybrid nonprofit + separately governed trading entity | Independent charitable research plus commercial products/services; potential cross-subsidy | Additional cost and complexity, conflicts, IP/licensing, transactions, governance and legal constraints | Undecided |
| Fiscal host / existing institution + technology collaboration | Funding and contracting through an established lawful institution | Contract terms, ownership, sponsor duties, eligibility, research independence and portability | Undecided |

**None** is currently the preferred or ratified option. The UK as a potential legal domicile **does not** confine beneficiaries or research to the UK; relevant foreign-country registration and financial/ethics laws require per-country checks. A social enterprise is not automatically a particular UK legal form.

## Commercial hypotheses (to validate, not to claim as products)

1. An ethical, institution-paid **research infrastructure** offering provenance, reproducible evaluation workflows, secure compliant data interfaces or reporting, with an open-source core.
2. Research-methods implementation and independent auditing **contracts** with universities, public bodies, NGOs or foundations.
3. Hosted tools/support and consulting where organisations pay and economically vulnerable participants are not charged to secure their basic legal entitlements.
4. Impact-based contracts only if measurement is genuinely independent, attributable, auditable and does **not** incentivise exclusion, metric gaming or coercion.

**No customer demand, revenue, partner, contract, investor, grant eligibility, profitability or unit economics have been established.** Do not equate software usage, grant revenue or service enrolments with validated benefits to people.

## Required safeguards regardless of model
- Document genuinely independent outcomes evaluation, including negative/null effects, risk-of-bias, transparency and protection against sponsor capture.
- Avoid charging vulnerable people for access to statutory benefits; make accessibility and offline alternatives explicit.
- Ensure funding and investment conditions do not incentivise excluding complex or high-need populations or exploiting sensitive personal information.
- Treat open-source licence, intellectual-property rights and community trust as part of any future commercial licensing decision.
- Obtain actual jurisdiction-specific charity/corporate/tax/counsel review before founder employment, shareholder arrangements, gifts, restricted grants or revenue transfers.
