## How the site should surface these pages (design contract, not current UI)

- **Global footer "Legal & Trust":** Privacy, Cookies, Terms, Legal/Publisher, Copyright & Licences, Accessibility, Research Integrity.
- **Global footer "Help & Contact":** Help/FAQ, Contact when owned, Corrections, Governance, (conditional) Safeguarding.
- **About & Governance** should be discoverable from primary navigation once WEB-03.2 is approved. Non-technical visitors can quickly understand that HPI is a public-interest **working initiative, not an incorporated foundation**.
- Pages must work without JS where practical, have readable headings, one clear canonical URL, mobile/keyboard navigation, real content owner/review date/version and accurate site status. Preserve existing internal URLs under /library and /research as source trails.
- Cookie preference UI is **conditionally required by actual technologies used**; terms/legal pages may have jurisdiction-specific duties, not a universal one-size-fits-all statutory set.
- Avoid a "Contact us" form that silently collects case records. Implement a verified general channel *only when* staffed, secure, privacy-noticed and fit for purpose.

## Required prior work before final legal claims

1. Site data-flow inventory: host logs, request analytics, monitoring, security, GitHub/third-party disclosures, public issue forms, cookies/local storage and any transfers. Verify runtime behaviour rather than relying only on source scripts or CSP.
2. Assign an accountable **actual** publisher/controller and contact method, or record that a launch-critical decision is blocked. HPI's current unincorporated status must be accurate, and no fictitious charity/board is published.
3. Independent privacy/counsel review of actual personal-data processing and site terms. Contact options and safeguarding must be supervised by an authorised real human team.
4. Run accessibility automated and human assistive-technology tests, document exceptions and fixes. No blanket WCAG compliance badge.
5. Source licences, image/video attribution and third-party dataset rights must be checked against real copied content; no content-copy permission inferred from source links.
6. Code implementation and QA: footer across generated docs/home, sitemap/redirect, SEO and accessibility, storage-consent tests *if necessary*, no invented functionality, source and date fields.

## External standards guidance (as of 11 November 2026)

- [ICO — how to write a privacy notice and what goes in it](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/privacy-notices-and-cookies/how-to-write-a-privacy-notice-and-what-goes-in-it/), flagged by ICO as under review following Data (Use and Access) Act changes. Requires actual contacts, processing, lawful grounds, recipients, retention and rights, not generic copy.
- [ICO — cookie and similar technologies](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/cookies-and-similar-technologies/), notes guidance revision underway. Non-exempt storage/access technologies generally need informed active consent; strictly necessary exceptions exist.
- [ICO — when to provide privacy information](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/the-right-to-be-informed/when-should-we-provide-privacy-information/) confirms that a site link alone is not always sufficient; notices should be proactively drawn to affected people.
- [W3C — evaluating accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/): automatic tools cannot determine complete accessibility; qualified human assessment needed.
- [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/): technical design target, not verified compliance at this planning phase.
