# F0-02.4 — Ethical partners and affected-community consultation: operational DESIGN V0.1

**9 November 2026 · Internal, AI-assisted planning only.** T01–T04 are *design deliverables*, not completed consultations. HPI is unincorporated, has no signed host/partner, independent researcher, ethics review, data controller, appointed safeguarding lead, resident participant or secure case-management channel. **NO-GO for contact with vulnerable residents, recruitment, individual benefits advice, personal-data collection, fieldwork, payments and funded partnership.** Public professional-methods critiques via issue/PR do not constitute human-subject research.

**Scope:** Newham public-source feasibility, testing whether independent research adds anything beyond existing assistance. [Our Newham Money](https://www.newham.gov.uk/advice-support-benefits/newham-money) already provides free confidential benefits/income support. An HPI intervention duplicating it is *not* assumed useful. [NEW-02 official DWP source audit](NEW-02-newham-source-reconciliation-additionality-v0.2.md) records a 2023/24 local **78% administratively modelled household receipt** with **no verified HPI study cohort**, and the [F0-03.1 review challenge](F0-03-1-newham-independent-review-readiness-v0.2.md) leaves genuine independent review outstanding.

## T02 — Low-burden, non-extractive community consultation design (NOT YET STARTED)

The sequence is deliberately staged: **Stage A public methods review** → **Stage B protected feasibility agreement with lawful sponsor** → **Stage C human-approved community consultation** → **Stage D only then consider research approval**. An email invitation, survey, open signup or focus group aimed at claimants is *not* authorised today.

1. **Before an invitation:** appoint accountable sponsor, independent ethics/method oversight, local safeguarding lead/deputy, secure communication and grievance channels; approve legal purposes, cost compensation, no-detriment policy and a clear safe referral route to existing services. Local representatives must help design the questions and decide if consultation is appropriate.
2. **Recruitment/access plan to be reviewed, not enacted:** candidate channels include trusted pre-existing local support organisations with written agreements; *only after approval* offer in-person, telephone, paper/large-print, appropriate translated or interpreted, and non-digital options. Avoid public GitHub, unsolicited benefits case messages and digital-only recruitment.
3. **Low-burden format:** propose 20–30 minute small-group or individual methods feedback sessions, with opt-out and shorter alternative, accessibility breaks, no document upload, no automated assessment of benefit eligibility, no recording by default, and no expectation of application support or payment. **Durations and methods are illustrative, not commitments.**
4. **Question prompts, not eligibility collection:** Does this research question match experienced access barriers? What risks/harms of mistaken advice or duplicate outreach are missing? Which communication modes and accessible languages matter? How can an evaluator verify real benefit receipt *without* asking people to publicly disclose private records? What would make the research not worth their time?
5. **Autonomy and fairness:** genuine informed choice, withdrawal/no detriment, reasonable approved compensation without undue inducement, non-interference with ongoing benefits and existing council services; no proxy for a legal entitlement check. Include affected people who do **not** use digital services.
6. **Safe output:** publish only approved aggregate themes, uncertainty, potential negative/dissenting views, methods changes and no-go reasons. Suppress small cells and identifiable anecdotes, obtain quotation permission, and store any necessary source records only through an appointed secure controller outside HPI public GitHub/SolOS/HubOS.
7. **Stop rules:** participant distress, safeguarding disclosure with no capable response, inadvertent case-data collection, coercion, accessibility failure, adverse welfare effect, unexpected conflict, unauthorised data sharing, duplicate service pressure or lack of community usefulness → **STOP and refer appropriately through human-owned channels** (none currently active).

**Community benefit:** partners and community representatives should have meaningful influence over the question, burden, interpretation and decision to stop. A one-time vote or high participation count is not evidence of meaningful representation or human prosperity.

## T03 — UK legal-basis, consent and independent approvals: determine, do NOT presume

The [ICO guidance on research processing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/the-research-provisions/principles-and-grounds-for-processing/) stresses there is **no automatic research lawful basis**: UK GDPR Article 6 lawful basis must be specified for *each real processing purpose*; a valid Article 9 condition is additionally needed **only where special-category data are processed**, alongside any other applicable law. Ethical consent to take part **is separate** from a privacy-law legal basis. The ICO's research provisions remain under review following the Data (Use and Access) Act; law and guidance must be checked again by real qualified counsel/privacy teams at activation.

| Proposed information flow | Current permission | Future control before activation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public official aggregate/metadata only, no identification | **GO, subject to source licence and data accuracy** | Keep primary URLs, definitions, permissions, dates, and revision evidence |
| Public non-sensitive scientific critique by willing experts | **Documentation/research-method review only** | Transparent public repository rules, voluntary attribution, conflicts and no identifiable claimant information |
| Direct partner employee contacts | **NOT AUTHORISED by this plan** | Appoint HPI legal/organisational contact, purpose, minimised details, notices and lawful basis as appropriate |
| Community consultation with older residents | **NO-GO** | Sponsor, ethics/independent governance, safeguarding, protected channels, fair informed participation, accessibility and approved privacy conditions |
| Verifiable household eligibility, benefit decisions, awarded/paid records | **NO-GO** | Source-specific data/controller permissions, lawful basis and statutory confidentiality, DPIA assessment, purpose/minimisation/security/retention, controller/processor allocation and case-level consent/ethics where appropriate |
| Cross-border or third-party processing | **NO-GO** | Actual destination/purpose analysis and transfer mechanism, separate from ordinary entitlement to obtain records |

The UK [HRA decision guidance](https://www.hra.nhs.uk/approvals-amendments/what-approvals-do-i-need/) and [non-NHS research guidance](https://www.hra.nhs.uk/approvals-amendments/what-approvals-do-i-need/research-ethics-committee-review/non-nhs-research-projects/) require **context-specific jurisdictional/organisational determination**. A Newham benefits-access study outside NHS care is **not automatically** HRA/NHS research; nor does this exempt it from sponsor, safeguarding, legitimate ethics review or legal requirements. Only an actual accountable institution can decide applicability with competent advice.

**Pre-implementation mandatory evidence:** (1) legal sponsor/host and protocol owner; (2) approved exact research vs service-evaluation classification; (3) actual Article 6 basis for each purpose, other relevant confidentiality rules and Article 9 condition where applicable; (4) roles, processing register, DPIA need/decision, data access/sharing agreements and retention; (5) independent method/ethics and safeguarding decisions; (6) accessible informed participation plan where applicable. None is signed or verified.

Do not promise response times that HPI cannot meet.
