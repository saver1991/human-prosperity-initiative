# F0-05.1A — First Newham Pension Credit study concept V0.1

**7 November 2026 — INTERNAL PRE-PILOT DESIGN. NOT APPROVED FOR FIELDWORK.** No research sponsor, local delivery partner, independent methods reviewer, research ethics approval, institutional legal entity, approved participant data access, funding or actual intervention exists. This document is a **falsifiable idea** for future external review.

## Question and theory of change

**Problem:** Some pension-age Newham households may be eligible for Pension Credit without receiving it. Eligibility can be difficult to navigate, but **Our Newham Money already supports claimants**. Its 2024/25 council self-report describes 220 residents supported to claim and £748,590 additional income; neither result is an independently measured HPI impact.

**Falsifiable, conditional question:** If an independent partner establishes a lawful population of **eligible baseline nonrecipients**, could a precisely described *additional* accessible navigation service increase the share with **confirmed first Pension Credit payment**, compared with **existing support in full**, without imposing new burdens on other people?

**Mechanism hypothesis:** remove a well-defined extra friction (e.g., more accessible assistance with approved referrals), enabling eligible people to navigate existing statutory services. No individual is guaranteed entitlement, and **no benefit may be withheld or delayed** as part of a comparison.

**Null hypothesis:** no incremental difference in the proportion receiving the first confirmed benefit payment. We do **not** postulate a numerical effect, sample or success threshold from present data.

## Population, unit and competing designs (not yet selected)

**Setting:** London Borough of Newham (ONS code E09000025). Working target: low-income households with a pension-age member who could meet statutory Pension Credit eligibility. An independent lawful process would be needed to establish actual entitlement and baseline nonreceipt.

- **Analysis unit:** preferably the statutorily relevant **benefit unit**; household-level service data are not automatically equivalent. Exact eligibility date, new vs existing claims, household composition and overlapping family awards must be decided before any preregistration.
- **Option A — independently reviewed service-addition randomisation:** only a lawful, ethical, feasible assignment of *extra assistance* on top of unrestricted **standard services**, not of benefits or essential information themselves. This may be inappropriate, and no randomisation is approved.
- **Option B — prospective, controlled observational evaluation:** predefine comparison, selection bias, cointerventions, time trends, eligibility changes, clustering and spillovers. A simple before/after count cannot establish causality.
- **Option C — descriptive audit:** if lawful comparator or secure verified individual outcome linkage is unavailable, publish **only descriptive** and feasibility metrics and stop causal language. This is a valid outcome of study planning.

## Outcomes, harms and costs

| Role | Draft outcome | What it would require |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary HPI-B-01** | Share of independently verified **eligible baseline nonrecipient benefit units** with **confirmed first Pension Credit payment** by prespecified follow-up | Actual approved source and denominator, correct index eligibility and date, outcome ascertainment, uncertainty |
| Application funnel (process) | Supported, applied, decided, awarded, first payment, sustained benefit | Never equate council support to verified awards; include pending/denied cases |
| Secondary HPI-B-02 | Claimant time and expense, delays and repeated contacts | Approved low-burden accessible measurement and lawful collection |
| Material hardship | Change in access to essentials/financial wellbeing | Valid locally relevant instruments; not automatically inferred from cash transfer |
| Equity | Predefined lawful groups and non-digital/disability/language access | Independent necessity and re-identification review; small-cell safety and adequate precision |
| Harms | Incorrect advice, stigma, lost eligibility, exposure of information, stress, displacement of existing council support | Named independent escalation/stop process, not a self-certified HPI policy |
| Costs | Actual programme administration and resident burdens vs fiscal transfers and sustained benefit | Itemised costs; never treat total benefit value as net cost-effectiveness or causal gain |

**Illustrative follow-ups:** baseline, roughly 3 months and 6 months after a *hypothetical* service offer, to be revised by an approved local partner for real Pension Credit payment rules. Date, retention, losses, reversals, missingness, selective outcomes, attrition and practical burden must be pre-specified. **No actual follow-up has occurred.**

## Power, data rights and research independence

**No sample-size/power calculation is defensible today.** The locally eligible baseline denominator, number of verified awards, expected additive effect, meaningful threshold, variance, clustering, missingness, partner caseload and programme cost are unmeasured. Neither **46.7% Newham income-deprived people aged 60+** (MHCLG 2025) nor **62% GB Pension Credit take-up** (DWP FYE2025) is an approved local eligible study denominator.

All real participant or claims files, including pseudonymised files, must remain outside HPI's public GitHub, website, SolOS and HubOS. Any future handling requires a lawful accountable controller/processor, source-specific written DWP/council permissions, DPIA/ethics when appropriate, minimisation, retention, access control, safe disclosure and independent governance. **Do not approach residents or publicise an invitation as though an HPI study is open.**

## Independent go/no-go (currently every gate unmet)

1. Registered/accountable independent study sponsor and source-specific qualified statutory implementation partner.
2. Independent scientific review, one primary outcome, defensible counterfactual, bias and multiplicity plan, pre-registration and appropriate power/precision.
3. Verified local benefit-unit eligibility and actual payment ascertainment with lawful permissions.
4. Ethics, safeguarding, informed participation, grievance/stop mechanism and non-withholding of legal entitlements.
5. Protected non-extractive affected-community and real accessibility consultation, including non-digital options.
6. Transparent additionality versus **Our Newham Money**, including capacity diversion, harm and null/negative evidence.
7. Real budget, administration, security, retention, legal and implementation capacity.

**Decision:** **GO for concept review; NO-GO for intervention, recruitment, participant survey, claims advice, funding commitment or live evaluation.** An independent review may decide the highest-value result is **not** to start a new project if Newham's existing programme already answers the question.
