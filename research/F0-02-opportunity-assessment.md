# F0-02 — Rapid evidence scan & opportunity assessment

**Version:** 0.1 • **Date:** 5 November 2026 • **Status:** Initial desk scan completed; systematic review, partner mapping, local feasibility and final selection are **open**.

## Scope and limitations
This is an **illustrative, non-exhaustive initial scoping exercise**, not a systematic review, meta-analysis, evaluation of local service delivery, or proof an intervention will work. We searched for established public research and institutional reports across three candidate areas; the attached [ledger](evidence-ledger.csv) captures selected sources, claims and caveats. We have **not** interviewed affected people, consulted policymakers, obtained administrative data, assessed a specific jurisdiction or conducted a formal risk assessment.

### A. Social-benefit and service take-up
**Problem:** People entitled to assistance may not claim it due to awareness, administrative requirements, stigma, digital exclusion or complex processes.

**Evidence:** Daigneault et al. (2025) reviewed 93 behavioural interventions from 35 field-experiment studies and found heterogeneous results; evidence predominantly concerns large US programmes. Bendtsen (2026) reviewed 51 field experiments and 187 estimated effects; increasing *applications* was easier than increasing *actual benefit receipt*. A navigation or information tool alone must not be mistaken for real economic impact.

**Pilot hypothesis:** With a qualified local partner, compare approved assistance or outreach methods and measure **confirmed successful benefit receipt**, retained access and participant time/cost—not just clicks, forms started or self-reported awareness.

**Challenges:** Dependence on local eligibility rules and authorised service access; possible exclusion of people without digital literacy; privacy, stigma, and the need to avoid inaccurate benefits advice.

### B. Essential-cost affordability
**Problem:** Household energy and housing costs can erode living standards; the burden varies strongly by local housing stock, climate, pricing and support systems.

**Evidence:** IEA's *Household Energy Affordability* report (Feb 2026) reviews trends and over 120 tracked affordability policies introduced in 2025. **A count of policies is not a causal estimate of impact.** 3ie's evidence-gap-map collection also covers energy-efficiency interventions.

**Pilot hypothesis:** Explore a targeted, independently reviewed programme to improve take-up of existing energy support or proven efficiency measures, with household bill burden, thermal comfort, health and distributional effects as possible outcomes.

**Challenges:** Capital costs, building access, landlord-tenant split incentives, seasonality and significant local policy dependencies. Avoid assuming a subsidy or efficiency measure is always best.

### C. Economic opportunities and livelihoods
**Problem:** Insufficient access to quality jobs and reliable earnings prevents many households from escaping poverty.

**Evidence:** The World Bank's *Poverty, Prosperity, and Planet 2026* stresses quantity and quality of employment. Its 2024 *State of Economic Inclusion* synthesis describes combinations of cash, training, assets, coaching and market access, with uneven reach and context-dependent implementation. 3ie maps relevant skills, labour-market and livelihoods evidence.

**Pilot hypothesis:** Evaluate an existing partner programme on verified earnings, job quality, consumption, resilience and longer-term effects—not course enrolments alone.

**Challenges:** More expensive and slower to evaluate, local labour-market constraints, displacement effects, selection bias and difficulty attributing change to the programme.

## Preliminary comparative assessment

| Criterion | Benefit access | Essential costs | Economic opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Potential for a bounded first test | Promising where a partner has verified administrative outcomes | Possible, but local cost and housing constraints may dominate | Possible, often longer implementation and follow-up |
| Measurable primary outcome | Confirmed receipt of lawful support | Change in affordability burden and living conditions | Earnings, employment quality and sustained resilience |
| Existing evidence | Reviews of field experiments; mixed and context-specific | Policy overview and diverse intervention studies | Substantial literature and major international programmes |
| Main constraint | Eligibility, privacy, administrative access | Capital, seasonality, landlord and policy context | Funding, employer/market links and displacement |
| Additive contribution HPI could test | Open, replicable evaluation and inclusive navigation | Context-specific optimisation and transparent costing | Transferability, equity and longer-run effects |

**Working discovery lead: benefit/service take-up** — solely because a narrow question can sometimes be evaluated with fewer resources and a clear administrative outcome. This is **not** an evidence-based ranking of social importance or guaranteed effectiveness. It is reversible if a more suitable partner and strong unmet need emerge elsewhere. No programme or region has been selected.

## Proposed decision method

Use the charter's six criteria only after gathering local evidence: need severity (20%), impact evidence (20%), feasibility/cost (20%), ethics (15%), additionality (15%), measurable learning (10%). Score 1–5 with a source and uncertainty note for every criterion. If an ethically serious risk cannot be mitigated or lawful data access is absent, exclude the candidate regardless of score. **No numeric scores are assigned at this stage** to avoid false precision.

## Next research tasks / F0-02 exit criteria

1. Publish search protocol, inclusion/exclusion criteria and a reproducible evidence search log for all three domains.
2. Extract at least 10 high-relevance studies/reviews per domain where available; capture effect, comparator, country and methodological limitations.
3. Map existing providers and opportunities in 1–2 candidate jurisdictions, without contacting vulnerable populations or collecting their personal data.
4. Consult affected people and field experts through lawful, ethical partner-led processes.
5. Apply the rubric with independent reviewers; document disagreements and a go/no-go choice.
6. Write a pre-pilot concept note only after steps 1–5.
