# F0-03 — Prosperity measurement framework V0.1 (draft)

**Date:** 5 November 2026. **Status:** Proposed measurement architecture for independent review. No participant data, validated HPI index, implemented intervention, causal effect estimate or approved field study is claimed.

## Purpose and decision boundary

HPI's long-term ambition is broad, but each applied study must answer one narrow, falsifiable question using outcomes that affected communities recognise as meaningful. This document proposes a transparent outcome language and initial indicator catalogue. It does **not** create one weighted Human Prosperity Score: appropriate weights, thresholds, transferability, survey burden and validity must be justified before any index can be used.

The initial field of application remains unresolved. F0-02 evidence screening and jurisdictional/ethical feasibility must determine the first candidate problem. HPI must distinguish:

- **Reach:** an individual saw a notice, visited a page, applied, or entered a programme.
- **Verified access:** an eligible person actually received an entitlement or essential service.
- **Experienced benefit:** an outcome such as material security or time saved improved, compared credibly with what would otherwise have happened.
- **Durable impact:** differences remain at prespecified follow-up and do not simply shift costs, deprivation or burdens onto other people.

These levels must never be conflated. A bigger programme or source inventory is not evidence of more human prosperity.

## Source frameworks and what they do not establish

| Public framework | Relevant contribution | Boundary for HPI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [World Bank — measuring poverty](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/factsheet/2025/06/05/june-2025-update-to-global-poverty-lines) | International extreme-poverty line of US$3.00 per person per day using 2021 purchasing-power parities, updated June 2025; national poverty lines and locally appropriate measures for national policy design | The international line is a descriptive threshold, **not** a validated measure of intervention success in every jurisdiction; currency base, household equivalence, price year and data availability must be stated |
| [UNDP/OPHI — Global MPI](https://hdr.undp.org/content/2025-global-multidimensional-poverty-index-mpi) | Ten indicators across health, education and living standards, measuring overlapping household deprivations | MPI is not interchangeable with monetary poverty or HPI's five proposed domains. Its global weights and deprivation rules do not automatically describe a candidate local pilot or its causal impact |
| [OECD — How's Life? well-being framework](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/how-s-life_23089679.html) | Eleven current well-being dimensions, their distribution and four resources for future well-being | A dashboard of national/subgroup well-being is not a participant-level effectiveness measure. It must not be presented as a validated single HPI score |
| [OECD — Well-being and inequalities](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/how-s-life-2024_90ba854a-en/full-report/component-5.html) | Highlights gaps between demographic groups, inequality across the distribution and deprivation rates; distinguishes current outcomes from sustainability | Averages can conceal worsening conditions for a subgroup. Distributional and intergenerational effects require explicit reporting |

**Version rule:** Always record the version and year of a poverty line or measurement framework; do not splice 2017-PPP and 2021-PPP estimates, different survey designs or international and national thresholds without a justified bridge.

## Proposed five HPI prosperity dimensions

| Dimension | Initial measurable examples | Measurement pitfalls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Economic security | Real household resources relative to local prices; income/consumption volatility; difficulty meeting essential expenses; emergency resilience | Income is not the same as disposable resources; transfers, informal earnings, household size and inflation matter |
| 2. Essential needs | Verified receipt of eligible support; housing and energy cost burden; safe services; food insecurity | Programme take-up does not necessarily translate into reduced hardship; self-reported and administrative measures have different errors |
| 3. Health and learning | Affordable access and continuity of care; attendance and educational progression; self-reported functional limitations when appropriate | Sensitive data need strong consent, safeguarding and minimisation; simple utilisation can increase with worsened illness |
| 4. Opportunity | Sustained net earnings, job quality and employment stability; equitable access to practical choices | Training attendance is not employment; jobs may displace opportunities for others; selection bias and local labour demand matter |
| 5. Autonomy, fairness and sustainability | Time spent navigating services; ability to make meaningful choices; unequal treatment; burdens on others and future resources | Question wording and cultural interpretation vary; subjective surveys require a defensible, validated local instrument |

No dimension can compensate automatically for severe deprivation or avoidable harm in another. A future index, if justified, would require independently reviewed weights, explicit uncertainty and consent-based community involvement.

## Research-outcome hierarchy

Before any pilot, each study protocol must publish:

1. **One primary participant outcome**, with precise population and denominator, unit, direction of beneficial change, measurement source, timeline, minimal meaningful change if defensible, and attrition procedure.
2. Secondary participant outcomes representing other HPI dimensions, including negative/side effects.
3. **Equity and spillover outcomes** (who benefits, who might be excluded, and whether conditions worsen for others).
4. **Costs and sustainability:** direct implementation cost, time burden, marginal public expense and longer-term consequences.
5. A credible comparison strategy, statistical estimand, missing-data handling, power or feasibility justification, risk of bias and independent study oversight.

A headline metric of “people reached” cannot serve as a surrogate for actual impact.

## Three candidate study measurement packages

These packages are **templates**, not approved research protocols or decisions about the first pilot.

### A. Access to existing benefits or services

- **Candidate primary outcome:** proportion of independently confirmed eligible participants who actually receive the specified entitlement within a prespecified follow-up window. Document how eligibility is established, how “received” is verified, and whether the comparison uses intention-to-treat or another justified estimand.
- **Secondary:** time to receipt, legitimate benefits paid, claimant time/financial costs, duration of access, comprehension, complaints, incorrect eligibility advice and privacy harms.
- **Equity:** disability-access needs, digital exclusion, language needs, vulnerability to stigma, and unequal take-up between eligible subgroups; do not collect sensitive characteristics without ethical and legal review.
- **Failure case:** more applications without increased confirmed receipt, or increased receipt with greater administrative harm to others.

### B. Affordability of essentials

- **Candidate primary outcome:** prespecified household material-hardship reduction (for example, objectively verifiable real energy-bill burden where accurate and legally available), measured relative to actual household resources and a credible comparison group.
- **Secondary:** inability to afford heating or other essentials, indoor comfort where safely measured, arrears, household time burden, maintenance and energy savings relative to intervention cost.
- **Equity:** renters vs owners, disability-related heating needs, different household sizes and fuel types, where data can be collected legally.
- **Failure case:** projected energy savings that do not materialise, landlord benefit without tenant benefit, or harmful substitution into food/housing deprivation.

### C. Economic opportunities

- **Candidate primary outcome:** sustained, inflation-adjusted net earnings or suitable material consumption at a prespecified follow-up, not simply course completion or temporary employment placement.
- **Secondary:** job quality, earnings stability, working hours, control over work, household debt, health and wellbeing, employer retention, programme cost and potential displacement.
- **Equity:** access for groups facing labour-market exclusion, local labour-demand differences, seasonality and differing household caring responsibilities.
- **Failure case:** income increases for participants but equivalent losses among other workers, unsafe work or unmanageable debt.

## Reporting standard for causal claims

- Define study population and sampling frame **before** data collection, including eligibility, exclusions, recruitment and withdrawal.
- Record treatment, comparator, exposure, baseline, follow-up date and unit of analysis.
- Report absolute rates and denominators, effect estimate with appropriate interval of uncertainty (commonly 95% confidence intervals for frequentist analysis), attrition and missing data; avoid treating a nonsignificant result as proof of no effect.
- Pre-specify the primary outcome to reduce cherry-picking; keep exploratory outcomes labelled exploratory.
- Examine plausible negative effects, displacement/spillovers, subgroup inequalities and cost per **incremental verified benefit** (with uncertainty).
- For repeated measures, compare appropriately and avoid identifying before/after differences as causal effects without a credible counterfactual.
- Include an explicit evidence-strength assessment, risks of bias, source provenance, conflicts of interest and local transferability.
- Clearly distinguish **descriptive statistics**, **associations**, **predictions/simulations**, and **causal estimates**. SolOS or AI-generated simulations are not empirical validation.

## Indicator registry and decision gates

The [draft indicator catalogue](F0-03-indicator-catalog-v0.1.csv) defines units, denominators, candidate roles, sensitivity and limitations. All entries are **proposed**, not validated, and contain no individual records.

The future HPI study protocol must select a small, feasible subset, rather than collect everything. Indicators without defensible sources or appropriate privacy safeguards must not be used.
