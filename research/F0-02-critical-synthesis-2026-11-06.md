## Domain A — Access to eligible social benefits
**Summary:** Focusing on reducing compliance costs may increase **verified receipt** in some settings, but low-touch contact can fail despite massive sample sizes and outreach may change applications without changing payments.

- **Verified receipt gains in specific RCTs:** SNAP information-versus-assistance increased nine-month enrolment from 6% (status quo) to 11% and 18% respectively ([Finkelstein & Notowidigdo 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjz013)). Changing SNAP intake interview flexibility improved approval by **6 percentage points** in Los Angeles ([Giannella et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1257/pol.20220701)). French jobseeker meetings with social workers raised new benefit take-up **29% relative to control** while an online eligibility simulator was not effective ([Castell et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1257/pol.20220786)).
- **Counterevidence and endpoint mismatch:** Six EITC nudge trials covering approximately one million observations did **not** increase filings or claims ([Linos et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1257/pol.20200603)). In Spain's minimum-income RCT, in-person and social-media campaigns stimulated **applications**, but neither caused robust **approval** gains ([Domenella et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13209-026-00331-6)). Psychological framing increased *interest*, a different endpoint from actual payment ([De La Rosa et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2106357118)).
- **Review perspective:** 2025 systematic review (35 field studies) and 2026 meta-analysis (51 field studies) reach mixed but actionable conclusions; effects differ by application versus receipt and assistance intensity. Their included studies overlap; never add study counts together ([Daigneault et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/pam.70005); [Bendtsen 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/pam.70085)).
- **Implication for an eventual HPI question:** Can qualified, legally permitted navigation and follow-up raise **confirmed** receipt and net income *equitably* relative to usual assistance, at affordable cost? Need exact benefit eligibility, false-positive advice harms, privacy, disabled/offline access and service capacity before deciding anything.

## Domain B — Affordable essential energy and housing services
**Summary:** Targeted grants and structural heat improvements can help in some places, but **realised savings** and **adequate warmth** are different targets; projected energy efficiency alone is not a valid prosperity metric.

- **Unfavourable US result:** In evaluated Michigan weatherisation, investment cost was about **twice** realised private energy savings, and temperature improvement was not statistically established ([Fowlie et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjy005)). A systematic retrofit review found an average of roughly **7.2%** metered energy savings in its selected programme set, while effectiveness varied with evaluation quality ([Annual Review 2022](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-resource-111920-124353)).
- **Relevant favourable trials:** In seven low-income communities in New Zealand, randomised insulation improvements raised bedroom warmth around **0.5°C** with lower energy use ([Howden-Chapman et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.39070.573032.80)). Another randomised heater study reduced asthma symptoms and days absent from school, but its **lung-function primary outcome was not significantly improved** ([Howden-Chapman et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.a1411)).
- **Policy affordability heterogeneity:** Spain's social electricity tariff modestly reduced risk of energy poverty in a difference-in-differences evaluation ([Bagnoli & Bertoméu-Sánchez 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eneco.2021.105792)). A 2026 Brazilian discontinuity analysis found lower bills (**R$15.35/month**) but also lower electricity consumption; this may signal **underconsumption**, not sufficient warmth ([Brazil tariff study 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2026.115143)). Nonrandom Dutch and European studies suggest some local benefits, but causal inference is less secure.
- **Implication:** Need household expenditure as a fraction of available income, actual adequate energy services, comfort and harms. Capital cost, tenancy, grid and welfare systems strongly constrain replication.

## Domain C — Durable economic opportunities and livelihoods
**Summary:** Some targeted skills/asset packages improve earnings, but they often require intensive delivery and positive effects can **fade** over time or conceal job-related harms.

- **Mixed long-run evidence:** A Uganda grant RCT found earnings **+38% after four years** ([Blattman et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjt057)), yet the **same experiment** showed earnings convergence at **nine years** ([Blattman et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1257/aeri.20190224)). Count these as one independent trial plus follow-up, not two replications.
- **Potentially sustainable programmes:** Six-country graduation trials improved consumption in **five of six** sites, but involve a costly combined package ([Banerjee et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1260799)). Bangladesh research reported seven-year poverty and asset improvements ([Bandiera et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjx003)). Uganda training experiments show differing timing of benefits between firm-based and vocational training ([Alfonsi et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA15959)).
- **Adverse/null findings:** Ethiopia factory jobs produced no sustained one-year income gain and increased health problems, while entrepreneurship support had stronger incomes in that sample ([Blattman & Dercon 2018](https://www.gov.uk/research-for-development-outputs/the-impacts-of-industrial-and-entrepreneurial-work-on-income-and-health-experimental-evidence-from-ethiopia)). A lighter graduation package in Ethiopia improved some assets but **not consumption** ([JDE 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2025.103682)). Additional job-matching in a long Uganda trial sometimes worsened labour-market outcomes versus vocational training alone ([Bandiera et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1086/728429)).
- **Implication:** A credible study needs a durable income and quality-of-work endpoint, a feasible time horizon, market displacement screening, plausible partner capabilities, and total cost inclusive of supervision/inputs.

**Data integrity and evidence gaps:** A link and a peer-reviewed abstract are **not** a full validated evidence extraction. Numerical claims above are source-specific, not pooled HPI estimates. Confidence intervals are recorded only where checked; additional bias appraisal requires full text, dual human screening and explicit assessment instruments. The small purposive review may miss unpublished/null research and non-English trials; systematic searching is a future separate decision.
