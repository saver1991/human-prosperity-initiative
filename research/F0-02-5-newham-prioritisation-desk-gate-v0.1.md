# F0-02.5A — Newham: evidence-based preliminary prioritisation

**7 November 2026 — GO for public desk validation; NO-GO for research with people.** Newham is the founder-selected site for a bounded methods test, not an approved pilot. No independent expert/community review or source-specific participant-data permission has occurred.

## Evidence relevant to the selected population

- **46.7%** of Newham residents aged **60+** experienced income deprivation (MHCLG *English Indices of Deprivation 2025*, **IDAOPI Table 8**). Newham ranks third highest among English local authorities. This is **not** a Pension Credit-eligible population or individual eligibility calculation. [Official source](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/english-indices-of-deprivation-2025/english-indices-of-deprivation-2025-statistical-release).
- **39.0%** income deprivation across **all ages** in Newham (same official release, **Income Domain Table 7**). It is not directly interchangeable with the older-age rate.
- **62%** Pension Credit caseload take-up among entitled pensioner families in **Great Britain, FYE 2025**, *not Newham*. [DWP official statistics](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/income-related-benefits-estimates-of-take-up-financial-year-ending-2025/income-related-benefits-estimates-of-take-up-financial-year-ending-2025).
- **220** Newham residents supported to **claim** Pension Credit and **£748,590** additional income attributed by Newham Council to its **2024/25** support; self-reported service results, **not independent HPI impact** or independently verified unique awards. [Newham Ageing Well source](https://www.newham.gov.uk/downloads/file/9654/2024-25-you-said-together-we-did-5-).
- DWP **2023/24** potentially eligible local-authority modelled receipt tables exist, but the **Newham row has not been independently extracted and validated**; this methodology is not the same as the national FYE 2025 survey series. [DWP local publication](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/households-potentially-eligible-for-pension-credit-2023-to-2024).

The original [F0-03.2 measurement audit](F0-03-newham-pension-credit-desk-validation-v0.2.md) found **0 of 15 draft indicators directly measured** with their intended HPI participant-level definitions. Missing are the locally eligible numerator/denominator, actual award verification, approved follow-up, equity data, costs and credible counterfactual.

## Six criteria, no invented scores

The canonical F0-02.5 rubric remains **need 20%, impact evidence 20%, feasibility/cost 20%, ethics 15%, additionality 15%, measurable learning 10%**. **All numeric ratings, weighted total and jurisdiction rankings remain null** because independent input, comparable sources and cost estimates are absent. The [machine-readable decision register](https://human-prosperity-initiative.vercel.app/research-newham-prepilot-decision.json) records each criterion, source context and veto.

| Criterion | Current evidence | Critical uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Need 20% | Strong **contextual** older-age income deprivation (46.7%). | Pension Credit eligibility/unclaimed share unknown. |
| Impact evidence 20% | Reviews of benefits outreach suggest heterogeneous and context-dependent effects. | No independent Newham-specific additive effect. |
| Feasibility/cost 20% | Public data and HPI reproducibility toolchain exist. | Local data rights, partner, sponsor, budgets and comparator absent. |
| Ethics 15% | No participant records have been processed. | Older-person safeguarding, legal basis, disability/language access and non-digital participation review missing. |
| Additionality 15% | Our Newham Money already supports residents. | HPI involvement may duplicate current support and divert scarce resources. |
| Measurable learning 10% | Candidate HPI-B-01 (actual verified benefit receipt) is conceptually clear. | Verified eligible denominator, award numerator and follow-up absent. |

**Noncompensatory veto:** a high apparent need does not offset unapproved legal processing, unethical design, missing local verified outcome measurement or lack of additionality. An unconditional numerical score would be false precision.

## Falsification and alternatives

A genuine additional service improvement could be unnecessary if Newham's existing provision has a credible independent evaluation. The 220 supported claimants are a selected service cohort, not a random sample and not verified awards. The council-reported value of additional benefits is not programme spending or net social gain; claims may overlap other reports. Unmeasured harm to existing claimants, digital exclusion, incorrect advice or displaced support would count **against** intervention. **Recife** remains a different international comparison and **Naples** a reserve; neither was ranked or approved for fieldwork in this round.
